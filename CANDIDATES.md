## Assessment

1. Strongest: a recognizable domestic exchange becomes a dying husband's final review. The next husband implies the fatal consequence; the last word supplies the concrete cause. The poisoning is inferred, so the laugh is not guaranteed.
2. A strong absurd image, but the speaker's responsibility and the exact accident need more inference than ideal.
3. Clear fatal literalization, but giving someone your heart is a familiar joke mechanism.
4. Plausible dying bequest, weakened by the explanatory final sentence and a mild organ pun.
5. Dark family favoritism with a useful reversal, but the speaker's own cause of death is less immediate.
6. Memorable petty afterlife request, but more eccentric than laugh-out-loud and less clearly spoken at death.

## Selection and refinement

Select candidate 1. First implement its 105-character draft, verify the program, then make a separate wording revision.

Final tightened line (92 characters):

> Darling, if this is how you take criticism, your next husband is going to love your lasagna.

Replacing the bureaucratic phrase "handle constructive criticism" with "take criticism" makes the speech easier to say in one breath. "Darling" adds deadpan intimacy. The last word retains the reveal. Final scores: on subject 5, real laugh 4, twist 4, carved in stone 5, quotable 4. This is a subjective choice, not a claim about an actual judge score.
